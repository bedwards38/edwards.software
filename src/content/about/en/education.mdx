##  BS in Computer Science and Software Engineering
<Subtitle content="2020-2024" link={frontmatter.link}/>

I pursued a double major in computer science and software engineering at Rose-Hulman from September 2020 to May 2024.

### Background 
Before entering undergrad, I had little-to-no prior experience with software engineering; though, I always had a lingering feeling that it was the career I was meant for.
 
Having come from a rural town with a population just below 3000, there were very little opportunities for me to easily learn about CS before entering college. I tried a few online courses, but nothing ever really stuck. 

That all changed the moment I stepped foot on campus. I was quick to immerse myself into courses and the CSSE department to "catch up" to my peers, many of whom had *years* of experience with CSSE from high school. This required many sleepless nights, countless pots of coffee, and thousands of hours dedicated to a 2 year long intensive internship. 

The experience was especially grueling at times, but that never set me back. In fact, I actually found myself craving to learn more, no matter how complex the material was. I'll never forget when, halfway through freshman year, a professor told me: 
> "If you weren't already a CS major, I would've made you switch. This is where you belong."

There isn't anything I'd trade for my education or experience at RHIT. It's where I unlocked a part of myself that had been waiting to see the light of day.

### Relevant Course List
While the core CSSE curriculum was fundamental in shaping my skills/preferences as an engineer, these are the specific courses I've found to be the most relevant to my experience in the industry:

- Debugging - From Art to Science
- Software Design
- Software Construction & Evolution
- Software Project Management
- Software Requirements Engineering
- Network Security
- Cybercrime & Digital Forensics
- Programming Language Concepts
